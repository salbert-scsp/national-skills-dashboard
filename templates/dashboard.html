Overview

To understand how artificial intelligence is reshaping the workforce, we must look past job titles and measure the day-to-day transformation of actual workplace tasks. Rather than relying on macro labor statistics, this dashboard provides a granular view of the market's evolving technological makeup by quantifying the direct semantic overlap between daily workplace software skills and modern AI capabilities. With widespread AI adoption, workers face a more immediate question than "Will AI take my job?": "Which parts of my job will now be allocated to a machine?" This dashboard categorizes skills into three distinct buckets: AI Skills which build the engine, AI Enabling Skills that connect and run the engine, and Non-AI Skills represent standard operations and human domain expertise.

How to Read

Each skill is categorized into one of three AI-proximity buckets. AI Skills build the engine—specialized tools like machine learning frameworks and neural network libraries that few workers use directly but form the foundation of all AI systems. AI Enabling Skills connect and run the engine—cloud infrastructure, data pipelines, APIs, and embedded AI features in everyday software that enable workers without deep technical backgrounds to orchestrate AI workflows. Non-AI Skills represent standard operations and human domain expertise—legacy tools and tasks anchored to human judgment that remain largely unaffected by current AI developments. The bars show each skill's AI relevance within the current view, using an absolute banded scale: AI Skills score 0.70 to 1.00, AI Enabling Skills score 0.30 to 0.69, and Non-AI Skills score 0.00 to 0.29. Looking at which skills dominate a role or occupation helps identify where proactive upskilling toward AI-enabled workflows offers the highest strategic value.

    Job Specific Skill Tracker

    This view shows which skills dominate a specific job role. Even when a skill ranks highest in your occupation, its absolute AI Score tells you its true nature: if it is categorized as Non-AI (below 0.30), it means your role relies heavily on traditional, human-anchored capabilities despite that tool being central to your daily work. If your role's highest-ranked skills cluster in the AI Enabling range (0.30–0.69), your field is moderately intertwined with the AI ecosystem, and learning native AI features within your existing software stack will amplify your efficiency. If multiple skills rank as AI Skills (0.70+), your role is deeply embedded in the AI ecosystem. High scores within your specific job profile indicate tasks best approached by learning to orchestrate AI-assisted workflows, while lower-scoring skills highlight baseline domain expertise that remains stable across technological shifts.

      AI score over time

      Raw cosine similarity score at each 90-day snapshot, tracking changes in a single skill's AI relevance over time. Unnormalized, so the point is movement in the true measured value. The score updates when new reference material is found or when the skill's definition is refreshed by the pipeline.

      How we got the numbers

      This score measures how closely a skill connects to the modern AI ecosystem. The pipeline uses three specialized models to produce it:

      • Cross-Encoder Model (ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2): Evaluates the relevance of candidate definitions to the skill name, flagging low-confidence summaries for human review.
      • Source Auditing and Summarization (Gemini Flash Lite): Validates source credibility, distills skill definitions, and searches for evidence of native AI features embedded in tools.
      • Vector Embedder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 / ONNX Runtime): Projects skill names and definitions into 384-dimensional vector space for semantic comparison against AI anchor poles.

      Every approved skill's name and vetted definition are encoded as vectors, then measured against anchor poles representing different AI domains. The result is a raw cosine similarity score (0.00 to 1.00) that captures how semantically aligned the skill is to the AI ecosystem. Skills are then classified into three buckets using a deterministic decision tree: AI Skills (0.29+), AI Enabling Skills (0.14–0.29 with supporting infrastructure signals), and Non-AI Skills (below thresholds). The classification never changes as you filter the dashboard; only the visible ranking order changes.

      Vector Encoding and Normalization

      Skill names and definitions are tokenized and encoded into vectors using mean pooling over token embeddings. The resulting vectors are L2-normalized, which projects them onto a unit hypersphere. This reduces cosine similarity calculation to a simple dot product for fast comparison:

      similarity = A · B (for unit vectors)

      To prevent short skill names from producing high-variance scores, the system blends similarity measurements from both the full definition and the name using a 90/10 weighted average: 90% definition, 10% name. This ensures the broader context carries the signal.

      The AI Score reported in charts and tables is absolute: it never changes as you filter. Raw cosine scores are always displayed in tooltips and the detail panel for verification.

      Anchor Poles and Scoring

      The raw AI score is computed as a winner-take-all maximum against two primary anchor poles: AI Engineering (neural networks, model training, computational inference) and Generative AI (large language models, transformer architecture, prompt engineering). Taking the maximum ensures a strong signal in either text is not diluted by the other, and all vectors are measured against both poles using the 90/10 weighted blend described above.

      Additional anchors—Technical Backbone, ML Infrastructure, Embedded AI, and Contrast—measure how a skill relates to AI rather than how close it is. These are reported alongside the score but do not feed into the primary AI Score, preserving historical comparability. They live in ranges roughly from −0.09 to +0.48, which is why the detail drawer shows the range beside each value: a bare four-decimal figure without context invites misreading as a percentage.

      When a flagship version of a tool is identified, Embedded AI similarity takes the maximum of the blended score and a fresh measurement of the note about the tool's native AI features. An explicit confirmation also adds a flat 0.05 boost to the raw AI score.

      Whether a tool embeds AI is searched for, not scored

      The AI embedded? column is the one input on this dashboard that is not a measurement. It comes from a web search for the product followed by a reading of the results, and it is the only thing that decides the Non-Technical Embedded AI class. A confirmed finding also adds a flat 0.05 to the raw AI score, shown broken out in the detail drawer.

      It used to be scored, and that is why it changed. The Embedded AI similarity column measures how a definition reads against vocabulary like workspace, workflow, integration and automated, which is the vocabulary of almost any productivity or design tool. A third of every skill in the store cleared the old threshold, and the class it produced contained LaTeX, EndNote and a 2D drafting package. Most of these definitions were written before the features they were being judged on existed. The similarity is still shown, beside the tag, because the disagreement between them is informative.

      Not checked is a real answer and not a blank. A product nobody has searched for and a product searched and found to ship no AI features are different facts, and only one of them says anything about the product. Searches get rate limited, so an unchecked skill is treated exactly as a negative for classification and never counted as one.

      Classification Decision Tree

      Skills are classified using a deterministic, first-match-wins rule tree that evaluates the raw AI score and related measurements in order. The thresholds are:

      • AI_SKILL_THRESHOLD: 0.29 — raw score at or above this classifies as AI Skill (Rule 0), unless technical enabling signals indicate otherwise.
      • AI_ENGINEERING_FLOOR: 0.14 — used by Rule 3 (Conceptual AI) as a gate for skills that show AI engineering affinity even if below the primary threshold.
      • TECH_BASE_FLOOR: 0.255 — threshold for evaluating general technical backbone signals.

      The three classes returned are:

      • AI Skill. Direct machine learning engineering, neural network frameworks, deep learning libraries, and core AI infrastructure.
      • AI Enabling Skill. Cloud infrastructure, data pipelines, APIs, and embedded AI features that allow workers to orchestrate or deploy intelligent workflows without building models from scratch.
      • Not an AI Skill. Legacy workflows, domain tools, and human expertise that remain stable across technological shifts.
      A calibration note: neutral English text scores approximately 0.00 against the AI poles. Non-language software of ordinary specificity (without programming or technical prose) typically scores 0.10 to 0.14 on the AI poles—this range should be treated as noise and reflects general technical vocabulary rather than genuine AI relevance. Language-heavy text (documentation, code examples, API references) reads higher due to semantics shared with AI domains, not because the software is AI-adjacent.

      Human review

      A skill reaches these panels only after its reference page clears a cross-encoder relevance gate and its source passes a credibility audit, or after a human approves it in the review queue. Nothing is scored before that point.
